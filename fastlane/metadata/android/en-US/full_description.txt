Wi-Fi Notifier does exactly one thing: it tells you the name of the Wi-Fi
network your phone has connected to. There is no screen at all - the app lives
in the notification shade.

Two notifications:

* a silent ongoing one that always shows the network the phone is on right now,
  with a Stop action that shuts the watcher down;
* an alerting one, posted the moment the phone joins a network, with the name
  of that network as its text.

Reconnecting to a network you left alerts again; a system-initiated restart of
the app does not send a duplicate.

On the first launch the app asks for three permissions: notifications, nearby
Wi-Fi devices and location. Location is not used to determine your position:
Android classifies the name of the connected Wi-Fi network as location data and
reports "<unknown ssid>" without that permission. The watcher is a foreground
service typed location for the same reason - a "while in use" location
permission keeps working for a background service only when the service is
declared as a location service.

The app has no network access, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party
libraries: it builds against the Android SDK alone.
